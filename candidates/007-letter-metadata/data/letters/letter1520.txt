My dear Sir 
Immediately on receipt of your letter of the 7th of April, which by some mischance, must have been nearly two months at the road, I commenced operations relative to the Travels of Evlia Effendi. The prospectus was translated into English and handed over by myself to the bookseller, who at present is the most extensive publisher especially in the line you have chosen, of any of the profession in London. At the same time that I forwarded to him the Intelligenzblatt, I made to him in writing a proposition for the purchase of the copyright of the Travels, but I think it is necessary to inform you that I did not confine myself exactly to the term mentioned in your letter; but in addition to the six hundred guineas fixed upon as the sum with which you should be content demanded for you 200 other guineas as a second premiums upon the publisher selling seven hundred copies, or, in other words, immediately after the sale of the first edition.

I was inclined to make this increased demand, first, because I thought that if the Travels corresponded at all with the synopsis of their contents, they were fairly worth more than the money mentioned in your letter, and secondly, because having offered the publisher to undertake the revision of the translation and all other editorial duties, myself, and gratuitously, I felt that you a right to a larger remuneration, than would have been the case if the publisher had been obliged to provide a revisor and editor of the work at his own expense. Now the answer I have received from the bookseller, whose name is Murray, is that he should wait for myself and a friend of his to see that portion of the manuscript which you have already finished, previously to entering into an engagement for the purchase of the Travels. I collect from your letter that you have no objection to this inspection and as I feel certain, (if as I said before

Mr. M who was kind enough to promise he could take charge of any parquet or parcel for me – at any ate be good enough to give me a line as early as convenient, respecting your resolves upon this subject. It will give me great pleasure if I can be of the least service in forwarding your views either in this or any other affair in which you may have to communicate with London. I must not forget to tell you that the mines have not yet appeared in the Edinburgh or Quartely review and I have not thought it worthwhile to introduce to my other journal of inferior notice – If they should be noticed in either of the two I will take care to send you the pages. The prospectus of the Travels does not come under the description of the materials which are admitted in the English Reviews and I think, indeed, that your object will be better answered by saying nothing about the contents until the work itself is in the publishers’ hands and about to appear. When your bargain is concluded I will take care that due notice shall be given of the publication of the Travels. Pray tell Countess Purgstall when you unite to her that her brother Mr. Cranstoun informed me in last February that he had quit send her a supply of arrow root, and that I will again send her some more. Inform the Countess Rzewuski  that her books were long since dispatched but not in a box but by private hand. Present my best compliments to her and Prince de Ligne. Believe me truly your obliged servant

John Hobhouse.



A Monsieur

Monsieur de Hammer

Premier Interprète de la Cour Imperiale 

á Vienne
