Royal Asiatic House, 14 Grafton Street, Bond Street
London 28th Oct 1834
My dear Sir,
Nothing but a long indisposition, from which I am just recovering could have excused me for so long a delay in acknowledging your numerous and valuable communications. Accept then, I beg, at once my sincere thanks for them all. The edition of Gul u Bulbul is truly beautiful. The type is certainly the best that has ever been attempted in that style. You will perhaps think my taste is bad, but in a printed books I prefer seeing a good Neskh  type. Hitherto however no such type has been cast in Europe. The only thing that has pleased me in that way is the Persian type in which , I think, at Tabriz. It is much superior to that of the Gulistan.

On one account I am particularly sorry that I have not been able to write to you sooner; viz that I might have acknowledged your Article on Turkish Literature, and have informed you that I some time ago transmitted to you through the house of M.M. Eccles & Co. of Vienna the sum of (£ 20) Twenty Pounds, as was agreed upon with the Editor of the Athenaeum. This sum I trust you have safely received, and as I feel very anxious about it, I have to request that you will kindly apprise me of its receipt as early as convenient. The Article has been translated and will shortly be published. The Editor is highly pleased with it. It will appear in French at the same time in Paris. 

You are no doubt aware that the First livraison of your Evliya has been published this summer. The periodical press has spoken very highly of it. You are entitled to 25 copies of it. Of these I have sent two to the Austrian Embassy to be forwarded to you. The remainder I keep until I receive your instructions as to how they are disposed of. I present one to Mr. Keene.

Begging you will excuse the shortness of this letter and hoping to hear from you soon. I remain My dear Sir your most obedient Servant

James Mitchell
